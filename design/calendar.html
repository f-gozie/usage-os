Board · proposed surface

Calendar

One surface, three rungs. Year → Month → Day. You zoom until the answer is there and then you stop. Each rung is honest about the days nothing was watching.

1 · The ladder

One nav tab — Calendar — not two. Year and Month are zoom levels of the same thing, and Day already exists. The breadcrumb is the whole navigation model: it is always visible, always shows where you are, and every rung above you is one click.

The rule that shapes everything The mark has to change at each rung, because the dial does not survive the shrink. MiniDial draws its R77 ink casing at 1 unit either side of an 80-unit viewBox — 1.25% of rendered size — so the casing is a true 1px only at an 80px dial. Month cells come out 126px wide, which fits. A year cell is ~29px. At that size the casing is 0.36px, the arcs are sub-pixel, and a 15-minute run is invisible. So Year gets a different mark, chosen to keep the one rule that matters: colour still encodes category and nothing else.

2 · Year

Two layouts, one toggle — see the note below the grid. Either way: one slim stacked bar per day. Height is hours, segments are categories, and that is the entire vocabulary. Height is geometry, not a second colour channel — which is what keeps this from becoming the contribution graph. Click a bar for that Day; click a month name for that Month.

Asks you to decide Which layout? Flip the toggle above the grid — same data, same mark, two readings. Month rows gives you the year as one continuous run: you see a stretch of heavy weeks, a holiday, the month you were ill, and the eye travels left to right without resetting. Wall calendar gives you day-of-week alignment: weekends land in their own columns and your week-shape becomes obvious, at the cost of that continuity — twelve small grids read as twelve things, not one year. Rows is the better instrument; wall is the more familiar object. I'd ship wall, because a surface called Calendar that doesn't look like one has to justify itself every time you open it, and this one doesn't have to.
Asks you to decide What does full bar height mean? Boarded as a fixed 12h ceiling, so a bar means the same thing in January and in July and the year is honestly comparable to itself. The alternative — scaling to your busiest day — makes every year look equally full and quietly lies. A third option is a fixed ceiling you can’t exceed visually but that clips: drag the slider.
Deliberately absent No year total. No “best month”, no monthly averages, no month-over-month anything. This is a field to look at, not a season to beat — and “long-range trends” is the part of the vision’s out-of-scope line that still binds, even once “month view” stops.

3 · What a year cell can say

Five states, and the two that matter most are the middle pair. A day you barely used the Mac and a day the app was dead are opposite facts and get opposite marks: a stub at the baseline versus a hollow slot at full height.

4 · Month — the middle rung

Here the dial comes back, at the size that keeps R77 honest. Measured in the browser: an 80px dial gives 126×130 cells inside a 984px frame, so the compliant size fits the existing window with room to spare. The cost is height — five rows run 678px and a six-row month about 813px, so the month scrolls. Scroll it, or break R77.

5 · Left open on purpose

Open Week start. The shipped weekDays() is Sunday-first; these boards draw Monday-first. They must agree, and that is a one-line decision I did not make for you.
Open Is Calendar one tab or two? Boarded as one tab with Year and Month as rungs, which keeps the nav at five. The alternative is a separate Month tab and no Year at all. The breadcrumb is what makes one tab work; if it feels thin in the real app, splitting later is cheap.
Not boarded Keyboard traversal of a 365-cell grid, the focus ring, and what a screen reader announces per bar. A year grid is the hardest accessibility surface in the app and it needs its own pass before any of this is buildable.
Now backed by real data The “not recorded” marks were drawing a fact the app could not know. That shipped: D72 adds a watch_coverage row per app run, and the read model hands every surface unwatched_secs. One caveat these boards must respect — days before that migration are unknowable, not failures, and have to render as ordinary days rather than as hatched ones.
